# Restart — Chapter Study Guide
> Gordon Korman · Narrators, summaries, highlights, important lessons, and review questions for all 30 chapters.

## 1 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Characters involved
Chase Ambrose; Tina and Frank Ambrose; Johnny; Corinne; Helene; Dr. Cooperman.
### Summary
Chase wakes in the hospital after falling from his roof and cannot recognize his mother, Tina, or his older brother, Johnny. Dr. Cooperman diagnoses amnesia; Chase's only clear memory is a little girl in a blue dress. At home, his father, Frank, praises his athletic success and dismisses the doctor's caution, while Tina insists he rest. Frank's wife, Corinne, and their daughter, Helene, seem uncomfortable around Chase. Helene is not the girl he remembers. His trophies and a photograph of himself smashing pumpkins with two friends make him question what kind of person he used to be.
### Highlights
- Tina and Johnny support Chase at the hospital as Dr. Cooperman explains his memory loss.
- Frank celebrates Chase's athletic reputation, but Corinne's distance and Helene's fear suggest a different side of his character.
- Chase cannot identify the girl in blue; his trophies and pumpkin-smashing photograph provide conflicting clues about his past.
### Important lesson
Achievements and popularity do not reveal whether someone treats others well; character shows in behavior.
### Review question
What does Chase remember, and what clues suggest he might not have been a kind person?
### Answer guide
He remembers an image of a blond girl in a blue dress, but not his personal history. Helene's fear of him and the photograph of the delighted pumpkin-smashers suggest that his athletic success may have hidden cruel behavior.

## 2 Shoshanna Weber
### Narrator
Shoshanna Weber
### Characters involved
Shoshanna and Joel Weber; Chase; Aaron; Bear; Mr. and Mrs. Weber; Hugo; Mauricia.
### Summary
Shoshanna explains that Chase, Aaron, and Bear bullied her twin brother, Joel, a talented pianist. Their attacks included setting off cherry bombs inside a piano during his performance, and the Webers eventually sent Joel to boarding school. At the frozen yogurt shop, Hugo and Mauricia ask about Joel, reminding Shoshanna how much she misses him. When Chase smiles at her, she reads it as mockery rather than the friendly greeting he intends and dumps yogurt over his head.
### Highlights
- Chase, Aaron, and Bear's treatment of Joel forces the Weber family to send him away to school.
- Hugo and Mauricia's questions bring Joel's absence into Shoshanna's outing at the yogurt shop.
- Shoshanna responds angrily to Chase's smile because she remembers the harm that he has forgotten.
### Important lesson
Forgetting the harm you caused does not erase its effects on others; understanding their perspective is essential.
### Review question
Why does Chase's smile mean something completely different to Shoshanna than it does to him?
### Answer guide
Chase does not recognize Shoshanna and intends a friendly greeting. She remembers his sustained bullying of Joel and reads the smile as an insult. Their different knowledge explains their opposite interpretations.

## 3 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Characters involved
Chase; Tina Ambrose; Aaron; Bear; Dr. Fitzwallace; the football teammates and other students.
### Summary
Tina drives Chase to Hiawassee Middle School and warns that he may hear surprising things about his past. His football teammates welcome him enthusiastically, while other students shrink away. Aaron and Bear, whom he recognizes from the pumpkin-smashing photograph, make room for him by intimidating another student. Chase tells them about his amnesia. Principal Dr. Fitzwallace later encourages him to see his future as a blank canvas, leaving Chase wondering why rebuilding himself would be necessary.
### Highlights
- Tina prepares Chase for his return, but his teammates' enthusiasm contrasts with other students' fear.
- Aaron and Bear reveal their intimidating behavior before Chase explains that he cannot remember his past.
- Dr. Fitzwallace offers Chase a fresh start rather than urging him to recover his old identity.
### Important lesson
A second chance becomes meaningful when you choose better actions instead of simply returning to familiar habits.
### Review question
What does the principal mean when he describes Chase's future as a blank canvas?
### Answer guide
The principal sees an opportunity for Chase to develop better behavior instead of returning to his bullying. Chase initially wants to recover who he was; Fitzwallace encourages him to consider who he could become.

## 4 Brendan Espinoza
### Narrator
Brendan Espinoza
### Characters involved
Brendan; Chase; Aaron; Bear; Shoshanna; Brittany Vandervelde; Latisha Butz; Jordan McDaniel; Joel, whose bullying Brendan recalls.
### Summary
Brendan films Brittany Vandervelde, Latisha Butz, and Jordan McDaniel for a mock nature documentary about the cafeteria. He fears Chase because of his bullying and remembers the harm done to Joel. When Chase unexpectedly sits beside him at lunch, Brendan helps cut his food because Chase's injured arm makes eating difficult; Shoshanna passes and looks disapproving. Aaron and Bear interrupt and humiliate Brendan by pulling away his chair. Chase initially leaves with them, but returns to help Brendan up and apologize, giving Brendan a reason to question whether Chase has changed.
### Highlights
- Brendan films Brittany, Latisha, and Jordan, then helps Chase with lunch despite remembering his bullying of Joel; Shoshanna disapproves.
- Aaron and Bear turn the encounter into a cruel joke by pulling away Brendan's chair.
- Chase returns to help Brendan and apologize instead of simply following Aaron and Bear.
### Important lesson
Small acts of kindness can challenge a harmful reputation and begin building trust.
### Review question
How does Chase's behavior differ from Aaron and Bear's behavior?
### Answer guide
Chase treats Brendan as a possible companion and helps him after the fall. Aaron and Bear treat him as entertainment and use humiliation to assert power. Chase's apology provides early evidence that he is behaving differently.

## 5 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Characters involved
Chase; Helene; Corinne; Frank and Tina Ambrose; Aaron; Bear; Dr. Cooperman and Dr. Fitzwallace, discussed.
### Summary
Chase catches Helene when she falls at a playground, but she becomes terrified when she recognizes him. Corinne thanks him while acknowledging her daughter's fear. Frank dismisses medical caution and urges Chase back toward football. At school, Aaron and Bear question his returning memories and reveal their community-service sentence for the piano attack, which they treat as harmless fun. When Chase confronts Tina, she explains the danger to the musician and audience and why Dr. Fitzwallace involved the police. Chase struggles to reconcile his mother's account with his friends' excuses.
### Highlights
- Helene recoils from Chase after he rescues her; Corinne confirms that she is afraid of him.
- Frank's pressure to play football conflicts with Dr. Cooperman's restrictions and Tina's concern.
- Aaron and Bear minimize the piano attack, while Tina explains the panic and danger that led Dr. Fitzwallace to call the police.
### Important lesson
Judge accounts of wrongdoing by their effects on the people harmed, especially when friends try to excuse it.
### Review question
Why is it difficult for Chase to decide whose account of his past to trust?
### Answer guide
He cannot check their claims against his own memories. His friends minimize their wrongdoing, while his mother previously withheld upsetting information. He must evaluate people's motives and compare their stories with the fear he sees in others.

## 6 Brendan Espinoza
### Narrator
Brendan Espinoza
### Characters involved
Brendan; Chase; Hugo; Shoshanna; Barton; Mauricia; the car-wash manager.
### Summary
Brendan cannot persuade Hugo, Shoshanna, Barton, or Mauricia to help film his dangerous tricycle ride through a car wash. He recruits Chase instead. They enjoy working together, and Chase proves skilled with a camera while Brendan struggles through the machinery. The manager initially suspects Chase of causing trouble, but recognizes Brendan and accepts his explanation and responsibility. Brendan's good reputation helps them avoid more serious consequences. Impressed by Chase's friendliness and talent, Brendan invites him to video club.
### Highlights
- Hugo, Shoshanna, Barton, and Mauricia refuse Brendan's plan, leaving Chase to become his filming partner.
- Brendan rides through the car wash while Chase captures the stunt and shows a talent for camerawork.
- The manager trusts Brendan's reputation more than Chase's; afterward, Brendan invites Chase to video club.
### Important lesson
A reputation earned through kindness and responsibility can have more lasting value than popularity.
### Review question
How does the car-wash incident show the importance of reputation?
### Answer guide
The manager assumes Chase caused the dangerous stunt because of his history. When Brendan accepts responsibility, the manager reacts more leniently because he knows Brendan as a good student. Chase notices that Brendan's reputation has real value.

## 7 Shoshanna Weber
### Narrator
Shoshanna Weber
### Characters involved
Shoshanna; Chase; Brendan; Ms. DeLeo; Hugo; Mauricia; Joel, contacted by message; Aaron, Bear, Joey, and Landon, discussed.
### Summary
Brendan screens his car-wash video for Ms. DeLeo, Shoshanna, Hugo, Mauricia, and the rest of video club. Their enthusiasm fades when Chase's name appears in the credits. Brendan defends his new friend, but Shoshanna objects because Chase helped drive Joel away. Ms. DeLeo insists that clubs are open to everyone and welcomes Chase. Hugo explains the video yearbook, and Chase is assigned sports coverage, including interviews with his difficult football teammates. Shoshanna later messages Joel but hides the fact that Chase has joined his old club.
### Highlights
- Brendan praises Chase's help, while Shoshanna and Mauricia react with disbelief and Hugo remains uneasy.
- Ms. DeLeo admits Chase and assigns sports coverage involving players such as Aaron, Bear, Joey, and Landon.
- Shoshanna discusses Chase's amnesia with Joel by message but conceals his membership in video club.
### Important lesson
Offering someone a chance to participate is a beginning; rebuilding trust still takes consistent good behavior.
### Review question
Why does Shoshanna struggle to accept Chase even when he behaves politely?
### Answer guide
She remembers the severe harm done to Joel and doubts that amnesia can change someone's character. Accepting Chase also feels disloyal to her brother, who remains unhappy away from home.

## 8 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Characters involved
Chase; Frank Ambrose; Helene; Brendan and Ms. DeLeo, whose filmmaking encouragement Chase recalls.
### Summary
Following Ms. DeLeo's sports assignment and Brendan's encouragement, Chase films a football scrimmage instead of playing. He joins his father, Frank, and Helene in the stands. Frank complains about the team's performance and presses Chase to return despite his injury restrictions. Chase later films Helene's dolls, briefly helping her feel comfortable around him. ### Highlights
- Ms. DeLeo's assignment and Brendan's confidence bring Chase to the game as a filmmaker.
- Frank pressures Chase to rejoin football while Helene offers a chance for a kinder family relationship.
- Filming Helene's dolls triggers Chase's memory of destroying her teddy bear and exposes his temptation to minimize the harm.
### Important lesson
Wanting approval can tempt you to minimize harm; honest reflection requires taking even small cruelties seriously.
### Review question
What does Chase's reaction to the teddy-bear memory reveal about his internal conflict?
### Answer guide
He is disturbed by his cruelty but then tells himself the toy did not really matter. His desire for football and his father's approval competes with his growing awareness of the harm he caused.

## 9 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Characters involved
Chase; Hugo; Aaron; Bear; Landon Rubio; Nurse Duncan; Mrs. Swanson; Julius Solway.
### Summary
Chase helps Hugo enter the football locker room after Aaron shuts him out. Players including Landon Rubio treat Hugo poorly, though Chase's presence makes interviews possible. Chase then voluntarily returns to Portland Street with Aaron and Bear and reports to Nurse Duncan. Their mockery of confused resident Mrs. Swanson bothers him. ### Highlights
- Hugo faces hostility from Aaron, Landon, and other football players while Chase helps him obtain interviews.
- Nurse Duncan welcomes Chase's voluntary service; Aaron and Bear ridicule Mrs. Swanson and keep her money.
- Chase replaces Mrs. Swanson's twenty dollars and meets Julius Solway, choosing kindness over his friends' example.
### Important lesson
Integrity means repairing harm even when it costs you something and brings no public recognition.
### Review question
How does replacing the money demonstrate that Chase's values are changing?
### Answer guide
He recognizes that accepting money from a confused resident exploits her. When his friends refuse to return it, he spends his own money to repair the harm without seeking praise. His conscience matters more than their approval.

## 10 Kimberly Tooley
### Narrator
Kimberly Tooley
### Characters involved
Kimberly; Chase; Brendan; Joey Petronus; Aaron; Bear; Shoshanna and Joel Weber.
### Summary
Kimberly watches football players bounce Brendan around in the hallway and initially treats it as entertainment. Chase stops the bullying by grabbing Joey Petronus, and Aaron and Bear intervene between their teammates. Chase identifies himself with video club, upsetting the players who expect his loyalty. Shoshanna's anger and the discussion of Joel help him understand that he took part in the same kind of cruelty. Kimberly, attracted to Chase and impressed by his actions, decides to join video club to spend time with him.
### Highlights
- Chase pulls Joey away from Brendan, while Aaron and Bear try to contain the conflict within the team.
- Shoshanna and Joel's history makes Chase confront the connection between this attack and his own bullying.
- Kimberly's interest in Chase draws her into video club and the circle of Brendan's friends.
### Important lesson
Standing up for someone may challenge your group's expectations, but loyalty should not require supporting cruelty.
### Review question
Why does Chase's use of “we” when referring to video club upset the football players?
### Answer guide
The pronoun places Chase alongside the video club members and separates him from the team in that moment. The players interpret his new loyalty as rejection, especially because they expect him to support them.

## 11 Aaron Hakimian
### Narrator
Aaron Hakimian
### Characters involved
Aaron; Bear; Chase; Nurse Duncan; Mrs. Bergland; Shoshanna and the video club, discussed.
### Summary
Aaron resents Chase's new friendships and cannot understand why he prefers video club to his old teammates. He and Bear notice that Nurse Duncan treats the helpful Chase warmly while remaining impatient with them. Before they can get answers about a valuable object Chase possesses, he is called away to take Mrs. Bergland to canasta. Aaron and Bear want what they consider their share but fear that explaining the secret to Chase could lead him to report them. His growing closeness to the elderly man connected to their secret increases their anxiety.
### Highlights
- Aaron and Bear interpret Chase's acceptance by video club as a rejection of their friendship.
- Nurse Duncan's approval and Chase's assistance to Mrs. Bergland contrast with Aaron and Bear's attitude toward service.
- Aaron reveals the pair's secret financial interest without identifying the object explicitly, increasing suspense around Chase's missing memories.
### Important lesson
Pressure presented as friendship can hide self-interest; consider what someone stands to gain from your choices.
### Review question
How does Aaron's narration reveal motives that Chase does not understand?
### Answer guide
Aaron reveals that their questions about Chase's memory are partly driven by a secret involving something valuable. Their concern is about possible exposure and profit as well as friendship. The object is not yet explicitly identified in this chapter.

## 12 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Characters involved
Chase; Tina; Johnny; Dr. Cooperman; Mrs. Kittredge; Helene; Corinne; Julius Solway; Nurse Duncan; Shoshanna; Brendan; Hugo; Kimberly; Ms. DeLeo.
### Summary
Photographs shown by Tina trigger Chase's memory of despising Johnny on his first day at college, a painful contrast with his brother's care after the accident. Dr. Cooperman continues to prohibit football. At Portland Street, Mrs. Kittredge teaches Chase about plants, giving him a way to help Tina, Helene, and Corinne. His friendship with Mr. Solway deepens, and Nurse Duncan explains that the veteran's medal may have been misplaced. In video club, Ms. DeLeo guides the yearbook work, Brendan supports Kimberly despite her weak camera skills, and Hugo encourages Shoshanna to consider Chase's suggestion that she interview Mr. Solway.
### Highlights
- Tina's photographs remind Chase of his cruelty to Johnny, while Dr. Cooperman maintains the football restriction.
- Mrs. Kittredge's gardening advice helps Chase contribute at home; Nurse Duncan and Mr. Solway discuss the missing medal.
- With Brendan, Kimberly, Hugo, and Ms. DeLeo around him in video club, Chase proposes Mr. Solway as Shoshanna's documentary subject.
### Important lesson
Listening and companionship can help people connect even when their ages and experiences are very different.
### Review question
What similarities connect Chase and Mr. Solway despite their different ages?
### Answer guide
Both are judged partly by past actions they cannot remember. Both can be isolated, and their friendship gives them connection and purpose. Their missing memories have different causes, so their experiences are parallel rather than identical.

Joel Weber

Joel; Shoshanna; Mr. and Mrs. Weber; Chase; Brendan; Ms. DeLeo; Kimberly; Dr. Fitzwallace; the video club.

Joel returns home but remains anxious and resents Shoshanna's enthusiasm for Chase. Dr. Fitzwallace offers a reassuring meeting, but Joel doubts that adults can prevent further bullying.
