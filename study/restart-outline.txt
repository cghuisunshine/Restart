# Restart — Chapter Study Guide
> Gordon Korman · Narrators, summaries, highlights, important lessons, and review questions for all 30 chapters.

## 1 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Summary
Chase wakes in the hospital after falling from his roof. He has amnesia and cannot recognize himself or his family. His only clear memory is a little girl wearing a blue dress. At home, his athletic trophies suggest success, but a photograph of him smashing pumpkins raises questions about his character.
### Highlights
- Chase wakes with amnesia after falling from his roof; the girl in a blue dress is his only clear memory.
- His trophies and a pumpkin-smashing photograph offer conflicting clues about his former character.
### Important lesson
Achievements and popularity do not reveal whether someone treats others well; character shows in behavior.
### Review question
What does Chase remember, and what clues suggest he might not have been a kind person?
### Answer guide
He remembers an image of a blond girl in a blue dress, but not his personal history. Helene's fear of him and the photograph of the delighted pumpkin-smashers suggest that his athletic success may have hidden cruel behavior.

## 2 Shoshanna Weber
### Narrator
Shoshanna Weber
### Summary
Shoshanna explains that Chase, Aaron, and Bear bullied her twin brother, Joel, until their parents sent him to boarding school. Their worst attack involved exploding cherry bombs inside a piano during his performance. When Chase smiles at her in a frozen yogurt shop, she interprets it as mockery and dumps yogurt over his head.
### Highlights
- Shoshanna reveals how Chase, Aaron, and Bear drove Joel away through sustained bullying.
- She dumps frozen yogurt over Chase's head after mistaking his friendly smile for mockery.
### Important lesson
Forgetting the harm you caused does not erase its effects on others; understanding their perspective is essential.
### Review question
Why does Chase's smile mean something completely different to Shoshanna than it does to him?
### Answer guide
Chase does not recognize Shoshanna and intends a friendly greeting. She remembers his sustained bullying of Joel and reads the smile as an insult. Their different knowledge explains their opposite interpretations.

## 3 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Summary
Chase returns to Hiawassee Middle School, where his football teammates welcome him enthusiastically while other students seem afraid. He tells Aaron and Bear about his amnesia. Principal Dr. Fitzwallace encourages him to treat his situation as an opportunity to rebuild himself, leaving Chase wondering what was wrong with his former self.
### Highlights
- Football teammates welcome Chase, but many other students react with fear.
- Dr. Fitzwallace encourages Chase to see his future as a blank canvas.
### Important lesson
A second chance becomes meaningful when you choose better actions instead of simply returning to familiar habits.
### Review question
What does the principal mean when he describes Chase's future as a blank canvas?
### Answer guide
The principal sees an opportunity for Chase to develop better behavior instead of returning to his bullying. Chase initially wants to recover who he was; Fitzwallace encourages him to consider who he could become.

## 4 Brendan Espinoza
### Narrator
Brendan Espinoza
### Summary
Brendan fears Chase because of his past bullying. When Chase unexpectedly sits with him at lunch, Brendan helps him cut his food because his injured arm makes eating difficult. Aaron and Bear humiliate Brendan by pulling away his chair, but Chase returns to help him up and apologize.
### Highlights
- Brendan helps Chase eat lunch despite fearing him.
- After Aaron and Bear pull away Brendan's chair, Chase helps him up and apologizes.
### Important lesson
Small acts of kindness can challenge a harmful reputation and begin building trust.
### Review question
How does Chase's behavior differ from Aaron and Bear's behavior?
### Answer guide
Chase treats Brendan as a possible companion and helps him after the fall. Aaron and Bear treat him as entertainment and use humiliation to assert power. Chase's apology provides early evidence that he is behaving differently.

## 5 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Summary
Chase catches his little half sister, Helene, when she falls at a playground, but she becomes terrified when she recognizes him. He discovers that he was sentenced to community service for the piano incident. Aaron and Bear dismiss it as harmless fun, while his mother explains the danger and panic it caused.
### Highlights
- Helene is terrified of Chase even after he catches her at the playground.
- Chase learns about his community-service sentence, while his friends minimize the piano attack.
### Important lesson
Judge accounts of wrongdoing by their effects on the people harmed, especially when friends try to excuse it.
### Review question
Why is it difficult for Chase to decide whose account of his past to trust?
### Answer guide
He cannot check their claims against his own memories. His friends minimize their wrongdoing, while his mother previously withheld upsetting information. He must evaluate people's motives and compare their stories with the fear he sees in others.

## 6 Brendan Espinoza
### Narrator
Brendan Espinoza
### Summary
When nobody else will help, Brendan recruits Chase to film him riding a tricycle through a car wash. They enjoy working together, and Chase proves talented with a camera. Brendan's good reputation helps them avoid serious trouble afterward. Impressed by Chase's friendliness and skill, Brendan invites him to video club.
### Highlights
- Chase films Brendan's tricycle ride through a car wash and discovers a talent for camerawork.
- Brendan's good reputation helps them avoid serious trouble, and he invites Chase to video club.
### Important lesson
A reputation earned through kindness and responsibility can have more lasting value than popularity.
### Review question
How does the car-wash incident show the importance of reputation?
### Answer guide
The manager assumes Chase caused the dangerous stunt because of his history. When Brendan accepts responsibility, the manager reacts more leniently because he knows Brendan as a good student. Chase notices that Brendan's reputation has real value.

## 7 Shoshanna Weber
### Narrator
Shoshanna Weber
### Summary
The video club loves Brendan's new video until its members discover that Chase helped make it. Shoshanna strongly opposes his membership, but Ms. DeLeo insists that school clubs are open to everyone. Chase joins and receives an assignment covering sports for the video yearbook. Shoshanna keeps this development from Joel.
### Highlights
- The club admires Brendan's video until it learns that Chase helped make it.
- Ms. DeLeo allows Chase to join despite Shoshanna's strong objections.
### Important lesson
Offering someone a chance to participate is a beginning; rebuilding trust still takes consistent good behavior.
### Review question
Why does Shoshanna struggle to accept Chase even when he behaves politely?
### Answer guide
She remembers the severe harm done to Joel and doubts that amnesia can change someone's character. Accepting Chase also feels disloyal to her brother, who remains unhappy away from home.

## 8 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Summary
Chase films a football scrimmage while his father pressures him to return to the team. He entertains Helene by filming her dolls. Her excitement triggers a disturbing memory: he once ripped the head off her teddy bear. Although troubled, Chase begins minimizing the incident as his enthusiasm for football returns.
### Highlights
- Chase films football and Helene's dolls while his father pushes him toward the team.
- He remembers destroying Helene's teddy bear, then begins excusing his cruelty.
### Important lesson
Wanting approval can tempt you to minimize harm; honest reflection requires taking even small cruelties seriously.
### Review question
What does Chase's reaction to the teddy-bear memory reveal about his internal conflict?
### Answer guide
He is disturbed by his cruelty but then tells himself the toy did not really matter. His desire for football and his father's approval competes with his growing awareness of the harm he caused.

## 9 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Summary
Chase helps Hugo interview the football players, then voluntarily returns to community service with Aaron and Bear. Their disrespect toward the elderly residents bothers him. After Bear takes twenty dollars from a confused resident, Chase secretly replaces it with his own money. He meets Julius Solway, a Korean War Medal of Honor recipient.
### Highlights
- Chase returns voluntarily to community service and is disturbed by his friends' disrespect.
- He replaces the twenty dollars Bear took and meets Medal of Honor recipient Julius Solway.

Integrity means repairing harm even when it costs you something and brings no public recognition.
### Review question
How does replacing the money demonstrate that Chase's values are changing?
### Answer guide
He recognizes that accepting money from a confused resident exploits her. When his friends refuse to return it, he spends his own money to repair the harm without seeking praise. His conscience matters more than their approval.

## 10 Kimberly Tooley
### Narrator
Kimberly Tooley
### Summary
Kimberly watches Chase stop the football players from bullying Brendan. His defense of the video club creates tension with his former teammates. Chase learns more about Joel and realizes he participated in the bullying. Kimberly, who has a crush on Chase, decides to join video club to spend time with him.
### Highlights
- Chase defends Brendan against football players and identifies himself with video club.
- He learns more about his role in bullying Joel; Kimberly joins the club to be near Chase.
### Important lesson
Standing up for someone may challenge your group's expectations, but loyalty should not require supporting cruelty.
### Review question
Why does Chase's use of “we” when referring to video club upset the football players?
### Answer guide
The pronoun places Chase alongside the video club members and separates him from the team in that moment. The players interpret his new loyalty as rejection, especially because they expect him to support them.

## 11 Aaron Hakimian
### Narrator
Aaron Hakimian
### Summary
Aaron feels hurt and confused by Chase's new personality and friendships. He and Bear worry about a valuable object Chase possesses that they consider partly theirs. They hesitate to confront him because revealing their secret could encourage him to report them. Chase's friendship with Mr. Solway especially worries them.
### Highlights
- Aaron describes feeling abandoned by Chase's new friendships.
- He and Bear worry that Chase's memory and friendship with Mr. Solway could expose a valuable secret.
### Important lesson
Pressure presented as friendship can hide self-interest; consider what someone stands to gain from your choices.
### Review question
How does Aaron's narration reveal motives that Chase does not understand?
### Answer guide
Aaron reveals that their questions about Chase's memory are partly driven by a secret involving something valuable. Their concern is about possible exposure and profit as well as friendship. The object is not yet explicitly identified in this chapter.

## 12 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Summary
Returning memories expose Chase's former cruelty, including his contempt for his older brother. His doctor continues to prohibit football. Chase grows closer to Mr. Solway, whose medal is missing and who cannot remember the wartime action that earned it. Chase recommends him as the subject of Shoshanna's video journalism contest entry.
### Highlights
- Chase confronts more memories of cruelty while his doctor continues to prohibit football.
- He learns that Mr. Solway's medal is missing and suggests him as Shoshanna's documentary subject.
### Important lesson
Listening and companionship can help people connect even when their ages and experiences are very different.
### Review question
What similarities connect Chase and Mr. Solway despite their different ages?
### Answer guide
Both are judged partly by past actions they cannot remember. Both can be isolated, and their friendship gives them connection and purpose. Their missing memories have different causes, so their experiences are parallel rather than identical.

## 13 Shoshanna Weber
### Narrator
Shoshanna Weber
### Summary
Shoshanna reluctantly investigates Chase's suggestion and visits Mr. Solway. He refuses to participate in her project until Chase arrives. Because Mr. Solway trusts Chase, he agrees to help them together. Shoshanna must accept a partnership with the person she blames for her brother's suffering.
### Highlights
- Mr. Solway initially refuses Shoshanna's interview request.
- His trust in Chase makes a joint documentary project possible.
### Important lesson
Working toward a shared goal can create an opportunity to reassess someone through firsthand experience.
### Review question
Why does Mr. Solway's trust in Chase create a problem for Shoshanna?
### Answer guide
She wants Mr. Solway as her subject, but his agreement depends on working with Chase too. She must cooperate with someone she distrusts or give up an excellent opportunity for her documentary.

## 14 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Summary
Chase's father proposes a second medical opinion that might allow him to play football. Chase and Shoshanna collect compelling interviews with Mr. Solway, whose spirits improve through their visits. They discover his empty medal case. Chase suspects theft and wonders whether Aaron and Bear are responsible.

The hidden empty case suggests that someone removed the medal rather than simply misplaced it. Chase has seen Bear exploit Mrs. Swanson and Aaron celebrate the money. Their greed makes theft seem plausible, although it does not prove guilt.

## 16 Shoshanna Weber
### Narrator
Shoshanna Weber
### Summary
Working with Chase changes Shoshanna's opinion of him. When her mother discovers them together, her parents react angrily because of his treatment of Joel. Shoshanna argues that Chase has truly changed. She suggests that Joel should return home because the main reason for sending him away may no longer exist.
### Highlights
- Shoshanna defends Chase when her parents discover their documentary partnership.
- She suggests that Joel may now be able to return home.
### Important lesson
Revising a judgment takes courage when new, consistent evidence challenges an understandable past belief.
### Review question
What makes Shoshanna willing to defend Chase to her parents?
### Answer guide
She has repeatedly seen his patience, kindness, and genuine friendship with Mr. Solway. His consistent behavior makes her believe the change is real. Her concern for Joel also leads her to question whether keeping him at boarding school is still necessary.

## 17 Joel Weber
### Narrator
Joel Weber
### Summary
Joel returns home but remains anxious about school and resentful of everyone's enthusiasm for Chase. He explains that bullying creates constant fear, even between attacks. At video club, Chase sincerely apologizes. Joel believes the amnesia and the apology are real, but that does not erase his anger or pain.
### Highlights
- Joel returns home but explains how bullying creates fear even between attacks.
- He believes Chase's apology is sincere while still feeling anger toward him.
### Important lesson
An apology does not erase trauma or create an obligation to forgive; recovery and trust take time.
### Review question
Why can Joel believe Chase has changed and still hate him?
### Answer guide
Joel still remembers the humiliation, fear, and disruption of his life. Chase's sincere regret does not undo those experiences or automatically restore trust. Recovery and forgiveness take their own time.

## 18 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Summary
A court document and Brendan's account of a lasting injury force Chase to confront the seriousness of his bullying. Hearing Joel play piano deepens his shame about targeting him. Small signs suggest the Webers are beginning to accept him. Chase and Shoshanna finish their documentary, Warrior, and she invites him into her home.

Trust is repaired through repeated actions, and small gestures of acceptance can mark meaningful progress.
### Review question
Why are the Webers' small gestures of acceptance so meaningful to Chase?
### Answer guide
Joel opens a window so Chase can hear his music, and later the family tolerates his presence inside. These gestures suggest that Chase is beginning to be seen as someone other than a threat. They offer hope without pretending the past never happened.

## 19 Bear Bratsky
### Narrator
Bear Bratsky
### Summary
The school celebrates Warrior at an assembly, making Bear and Aaron jealous and resentful. They interpret Chase's success as rejection of their friendship. His connection to Mr. Solway also threatens their hidden interests. Aaron proposes a plan to pull Chase back toward them.
### Highlights
- Warrior receives a standing ovation at a school assembly.
- Bear and Aaron resent Chase's success, and Aaron proposes a plan to draw him back.
### Important lesson
Resentment can distort how we describe events; compare a narrator's opinions with the evidence.
### Review question
How does Bear's account show that a narrator's opinions can distort events?
### Answer guide
He dismisses the documentary and assumes others were bored despite the standing ovation. He blames the Webers for community service rather than accepting responsibility for the bullying. His account reveals his attitude more reliably than it evaluates the film.

## 20 Brendan Espinoza

Brendan Espinoza

While Brendan films One Man Band with Joel and Kimberly, Aaron and Bear attack with fire extinguishers and damage instruments. They falsely claim Chase planned it. Summoned by Kimberly, Chase tries to stop them, but accidentally strikes Joel while struggling with Bear over an extinguisher. The arriving teachers see a scene that makes Chase appear guilty.

Which details make an attempted rescue look like a deliberate attack?

Chase is holding the extinguisher that struck Joel, the room is wrecked, and his former partners claim he planned it. The teachers arrive after the struggle and do not see how the injury happened. His reputation makes the misleading scene more believable.

## 21 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Summary
Aaron invents a story about an electrical fire to excuse the destruction. Chase supports the lie to escape punishment, sacrificing his honesty. Ms. DeLeo removes him from video club, and his new friends withdraw their trust. Another doctor clears him for football, but Chase realizes that the life he wants is the one he has just lost.

### Review question
Why is Chase responsible for damaging his friendships even though he did not plan the attack?
### Answer guide
He chooses to support a story that he knows is false. That choice protects Aaron and Bear while discrediting the people who were attacked. His friends have a valid reason to distrust the lie even though they misunderstand his role in Joel's injury.

Chase discovers that he still enjoys football, but refuses to restore his friendship with Aaron and Bear. They confront him violently and demand their share of Mr. Solway's medal. Chase accuses them of stealing it, only to learn that he was the thief. A returning memory confirms the accusation, and he vows to return it.

How does this discovery challenge Chase's division between his “old” and “new” selves?

He cannot treat his former cruelty as somebody else's history. The theft was his action, even though he would reject it now. His promise to return the medal begins turning remorse into responsibility.

Chase Ambrose

Chase recognizes the girl from his remaining memory in a painting being moved from his neighbors' house. Realizing he could have seen it from his roof, he climbs up and finds the hidden medal. How does the painting connect the memory mystery, the stolen medal, and Chase's accident?

The painting was visible from the roof where Chase hid the medal. After hiding it, he noticed his neighbor doing yoga and reached for his phone to take a mocking photograph. He slipped and fell, preserving the image of the painting as a fragment of that moment.

## 29 Chase Ambrose
### Narrator
Chase Ambrose
### Summary
At his hearing, Chase discovers that classmates, teachers, and even the Webers have come to support him. He refuses to guarantee that his former cruelty is gone forever because he cannot honestly know. Mr. Solway arrives and claims he loaned Chase the medal. With that intervention and the community's support, the judge dismisses the charge.

- Classmates, teachers, and the Webers support Chase at his hearing.

Credible change rests on honesty and sustained actions, including admitting that you must keep making better choices.
### Review question
How does Chase's honesty here contrast with his lie in the principal's office?
### Answer guide
At school, he says what will protect him from punishment. In court, he refuses to make a promise he cannot guarantee, even when it appears necessary to avoid detention. Mr. Solway's claim is an act of protection; the story has already established that Chase really stole the medal.

## 30 Brendan Espinoza
### Narrator
Brendan Espinoza

Chase returns to video club while also succeeding in football, and the groups become more connected. Warrior wins the national contest; Ms. DeLeo works to restore Shoshanna's credit after she previously removed her name. Kimberly and Brendan become a couple, and his tuba footage goes viral. A better identity can include your existing talents and interests when you pursue them with kindness and healthier relationships.

How does the ending show that Chase can build a new identity without abandoning all his former interests?

He plays football and contributes to video club, showing that those interests can coexist. Joel leads the pep band, and the club films the team, weakening old divisions. Chase's memories are still returning, but his present choices and friendships support his growth.
